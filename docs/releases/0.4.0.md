AI Juggler 0.4 adds the Floor, a live office in its own window that shows all your AI work at a glance and takes you to what needs you in one click.

## New

- **The Floor.** Open it with the Floor button in the header, Window › The Floor, the menu bar icon or ⌘⇧M. Each app is an area of an office, each session a person at a desk, each subagent a helper beside them, with your accounts and your Mac in the lobby. It opens on the floor with the app that most needs you.
- **Everything on the Floor means something.** Someone waving with a `!` is waiting on you; a `?` is probably stuck; smoke is a crash; someone asleep has hit a limit. A small fire marks an app using too much of the processor, a battery on an area's sign shows its account running low, and the lobby's rack shows how hard your Mac is working. Every state also has a symbol and a word, never colour alone. Click a person, a sign, the rack or the notice board and AI Juggler opens it.
- **Areas you can find again.** Each app's area has its own look, chosen once and kept, with the tool's logo and the account's initials printed on its floor and the app's name on its sign. Areas stay where they are: they grow with their sessions and only move forward when an app before them changes size. Each pool has a meeting room.
- **Move around from the trackpad or the keyboard.** Drag to pan, pinch to zoom, twist or use Q and E to turn. Tab moves between areas, N jumps to the next session waiting on you, Enter opens it, and 1 to 9 pick a floor.
- **Settings › The Floor:** open it when AI Juggler starts, keep it on top, how many apps fit on a floor, how much it moves, and Re-create the office… for a new look.
- **The Floor list** in the main window shows the same in words, by floor and app, and works with VoiceOver and the keyboard.
- **Report a bug from wherever it went wrong.** Help › Report a bug…, your menu, Settings and a failed switch open one sheet: your words first, then what goes with them (what AI Juggler showed, the versions, the last switch's steps, recent errors), each scrubbed on this computer and readable in full, each yours to leave out. Open the bug form fills in GitHub's form in your browser, where you can still change it before you submit. Nothing is sent by AI Juggler itself.
- **Debug mode.** Settings › Advanced › Debug mode writes down every step of each switch and launch (sizes, timings, the tools' own errors, never what's in a conversation) so a bug that comes and goes can be caught. It keeps at most 16 MB, turns itself off after seven days, and clears in one click.

## Improved

- **An ended session's page picks up where it left off.** **Resume** continues the same conversation on the same account, beside Relaunch for a fresh start; when it can't, it says why. Its ⋯ menu also has Replay, Archive and Copy the conversation's id, as History's rows do.
- **Notifications when you'd want them.** Settings › Notifications › When to tell me: a waiting session becomes a notification once it has waited 5 minutes (or 2, 10, 15, 30, or right away); Needs you and the bell still show it at once. An account warns you at 80 % used and again at 95 %, each once per window, or at the levels you choose. **Send test** shows one notification now, so you can check your system lets them through. If you relied on being told of a wait right away, choose Right away.
- **⌘K does things, not only goes places.** Before you type it shows what you opened last and the things you do most (New session, Settings, and Processes or Notifications when they have something). Type loosely — "wrk bil" finds the billing session on Work — across sessions, apps, projects, accounts (with their room left) and settings. → shows a preview beside the list, ⌘↩ goes to the app or starts a session on the account, and See all results opens History.
- **Settings you can search, keys you can change.** A search field heads Settings' sidebar (press `/`): type "mute" or "quiet hours" and it opens that setting. The new Keyboard pane lists every shortcut. Change… takes the next keys you press and refuses ones already taken, saying who has them; Reset puts one back, Reset all puts back every one, with Undo. The ? sheet, ⌘K and the tooltips show your keys.
- **Home shows only what you pick.** Chips over Needs you narrow it to Asks you, Probably stuck, Limits, To review or Sign-in. On a row, R opens what it asks, H continues it in another app, S snoozes it and E archives an ended one. Mark all reviewed clears Pick up where you left off in one go.
- **Leave out the start of a very long conversation when you switch.** Continue in another app has "Very long conversation? Leave out its start": carry only the turns from a day on, or only the last few. Everything is still the default, and the original conversation is never changed.
- **First run says how sure it is.** Each setup it finds says Found, Likely or Guessed, and where it came from ("Claude Code's usual folder", "set in your shell's settings"). ▸ Details shows how each part was worked out, a sign-in that may have expired says so, and **Look again** scans once more whenever you like, keeping your ticks.
- **Resize the list from the keyboard.** Tab to its edge and use ← and → (with ⇧ for bigger steps), Home and End. A screen reader hears its width.
- **The Floor costs next to nothing.** It draws only when something changes or moves, stops drawing when it's covered or minimised, and frees its memory when you close it. Nothing runs for it until you first open it.

## Fixed

- Esc now closes New project, even right after ⌘⇧N.
- The bell's key hint said ⌘⇧N (New project); the bell's key is ⌥⌘N.

## Known issues

- The Floor is on macOS only. Windows gets it in 0.6 and Linux in 0.7.
- **Going back to 0.3 from 0.4.** 0.4 updates AI Juggler's own data. The copy it keeps on the way makes going back possible: Settings › About & updates › Go back to an earlier version. That copy is from before the update, so what changed in 0.4, such as the Floor's layout, isn't in it. Your Claude and Codex folders aren't affected either way.
