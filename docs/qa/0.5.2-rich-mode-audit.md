# 0.5.2 rich-mode audit: Settings, the edit and create sheets, the pickers (AJ-E1558)

The maintainer on the 0.5.1 Nightly (2026-11-25): "monochrome hell again" on the Settings, edit and selection pages. The design system had the rule (D-126 rich mode, DESIGN-SYSTEM §2.2 and §2.3; PAT-COLOR §5.13; PAT-SURF §5.12; TGL-2, RC-2, PCK), but these surfaces weren't held to it.

**Method (Opus, 2026-11-25).** Every Settings pane (General, Desktop apps, The Floor, Accounts, Sessions, Switching, AI › Models, Agent access, Notifications, Privacy, Your Macs, Keyboard, Advanced), the App and Account pages' Settings tabs, every edit or create sheet (`Modal` callers and the sheets drawn with `DialogFrame`), and every picker (`Picker`, `ChoiceOption`, `RadioCard`, the menus' choice lines) read in code at 42aab7661 and looked at in the gallery's captures of that commit (65-settings-sections, 44, 62, 84, 89, 82, 97, 99-new-session, 98-pool-setup) in both themes, against the kit (`~/Dev/AIJuggler-design-refs/d126-rich-ui/kit-dark.png`, `notif-*.png`) and the calm r2 mock for the rail. `scripts/rich_mode_lint.py` (new, below) run on that commit found 40 components in 29 files that build a choice from neutral tokens alone; every one of those is in a finding here, and the by-eye pass added the rest.

**Rules checked.** Rich mode's dials (§2.2): an icon or logo on every choice, row and section that holds choices; the accent for selection and progress; choice cards and inputs a quiet edge, everything else tone. PAT-COLOR: one hue, one meaning, soft and strong steps; amber never a fill behind words. PAT-SURF: no outline on cards and areas, hover a fill, selected a fill (never only a ring or a dot). TGL-1/2 (a switch for what takes effect at once; every row its chip), RC-2/RC-3 (every card its icon; "Recommended" an accent tag), PCK (pickers of things carry their icons), TYP-1 (the type roles, no `text-xs`/`text-sm`), BTN-7 (no hand-made buttons), DLG anatomy (a making sheet's header icon tile).

## Usability reasoning, before building (PAT-USE), for the changed patterns

From the task (AJ-E1558), checked against each changed pattern:

1. **Who and what they expect.** Someone setting things up scans Settings for the section and the option they need. The pane tile, the row's chip and the option's icon are how they find it; the switch, the chip lit in the accent's soft step and the chosen fill are how they see what's on.
2. **A newcomer.** Each section and option carries its icon; the accent's soft step marks on, chosen and recommended; status keeps its own hue in soft steps (a warning's amber words on the neutral tone, a "Can copy" in green); tags and chips say state ("Recommended", "Override", "Can copy"); the chosen card, line or menu value is a fill, unmistakable at a glance.
3. **Its value, in one place.** The neutral rows are replaced in place, by the d126 kit's own pieces (`RowChip`, `RowLead`, `PickRow`, `ToggleRow`/`CheckRow`, `ChoiceOption`'s card, `SELECTED_FILL`, `SELECTED_CARD`, `Modal`'s icon tile); no second surface.
4. **One option, or empty.** A section with one row still reads rich: its chip and tone ("Your accounts", Desktop apps' empty list with its chip), never a bare line.
5. **System prompts.** None: these are looks; no new call to the system.
6. **What would surprise.** Watched for: colour without meaning (the pane tiles' five hues meant nothing, so they became one: the accent, navigation's hue), bright borders (none added; outlined boxes removed), contrast (the kit's tokens, AA in both themes; the gate's contrast spec), a selected state shown only by a ring or a dot (menus and radio lines now fill).

**The Needs you row (decided, AJ-E1557 amendment).** (1) A glance at the rail finds what needs you by its level's mark and words; (2) the App is named on the row's place line ("ai-juggler · Claude Code CLI · Work"; compact: after why); (3) no bullet-like dot before the row (the maintainer's "no bullet-like dots"); (4) a row that needs you always has its mark; (5) none; (6) the title doesn't move: the gutter stays, empty, as on every rail row.
