# 0.4 validation plan: every combination, and every real use

> Source: the maintainer, 2026-10-30, approving the 0.4 designs (D-62):
> "When you start implementing, it will be very important to validate and
> test every combination of those options. So make sure you
> systematically validate them, you should have a comprehensive process
> to validate them, not just in automated way, but from user workflow
> perspective. Real example use cases of how user would use it, this
> needs to be part of your plan for 0.4."
>
> **Why this exists.** 0.3's pools, its banners and its switch from the
> Claude app all passed their suites, and all failed in the maintainer's
> first minutes of real use: a pool that couldn't add the sessions people
> have, a close that took a minute, a switch that left the app it started
> in. Tests proved each part existed. Nothing proved a person could get
> their work done with it. This plan closes that gap for 0.4.
>
> The tasks that carry this work are E33 (AJ-E3301…AJ-E3306). Every build
> task in E30, E31 and E32 names the scenarios below that it must pass,
> and isn't done until they do.

## 1. What "validated" means

A 0.4 feature is validated when all four of these hold:

1. **Every combination that changes behaviour has run**, and its result
   matched an expectation worked out independently. The app's answer is
   checked against a separate reference model, never against itself.
2. **Every real use case it serves has been walked by hand, end to
   end**, in the Dev edition app, on realistic data. Each step is timed,
   and each element is scored for works, speed, clarity and layout.
3. **Every edge and failure path has been walked**, and each one says
   what happened and what to do next: refused, unreadable, offline,
   ended, sealed, 0 items, thousands of items.
4. **The scenarios pass again, together, on the release-candidate
   build.** The maintainer then uses it, and their findings are fixed in
   0.4.x.

A green suite on its own isn't validation. A hand walk on its own isn't
either. Both are needed, on the same scenarios.

## 2. The layers

| Layer | What it proves | How | Runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1 Model** | The rules are right for every combination | Exhaustive table tests of the pure logic in Rust: membership, precedence, talk decisions, sealing, the scope check, the Claude channel mapping, migration. A separate Python reference model computes the expected table, and both must agree row for row. | `just verify`, every push |
| **L2 Oracle on realistic data** | The app gives the right answer on data shaped like a real Mac | Scenario files (§6) are loaded into the replica HOME. The backend harness writes what the app resolves, and the reference model checks it: who is in which pool with what role and level, what delivers, holds or is refused, and every page's counts. This extends `scripts/qa/real_truth.py` and `qa_real_readonly.rs`. | Per PR touching E30–E32; nightly |
| **L3 Scripted journeys** | Every path on screen works, in both themes and both widths, fast | Playwright on the mock bridge, with one test per row of the pairwise tables (§4). Each test times click to visible response (≤ 100 ms feedback; ≤ 150 ms for a related tab from cache) and keeps captures for the gallery and critique. | `just preflight`; CI |
| **L4 Use-case walks** | A person gets their real work done | The scenarios in §5, done by hand in the Dev edition app on the lab, recorded step by step and scored | Before each build task is called done; again on the release candidate |
| **L5 Whole-day and load** | The features hold together over time | A day-in-the-life script (X1) with switching, usage, notifications, a restart and a crash in the middle; a 50-member pool; 1 000+ ended sessions; a cache cleared mid-walk | Release candidate |
| **L6 Maintainer use** | It solves the maintainer's problem | The beta, with findings going to 0.4.x as they come | After release |

## 3. The options, and how many combinations

### Pools

| Dimension | Values | n |
|---|---|---|

| How a session is matched | by name (Session) · its App · its Account · its Tool · its Mac · everyone in scope · not matched | 7 |
| Scope relation | in scope · outside scope | 2 |
| What it can do | full (Claude Code or Codex that AI Juggler started) · shell (OpenCode, Grok Build or Kimi Code, until their lab) · watched (own terminal, the Claude app's Code tab or chat, ChatGPT or IDE) | 3 |
| Role | Lead · Member · Reviewer · Observer | 4 |
| Level | Read · Work together · Full · Custom; the pool's level or the member's own | 4 × 2 |
| Talk, per role pair | allowed · ask first · blocked; 16 pairs; one connection overridden or not | 3 per pair × 4 |
| Sealed | on · off | 2 |
| Pools one session is in | 0 · 1 · 2 · 3+; also matched twice in one pool; one of them sealed | 6 shapes |

| Spans Macs | on · off (Mac members wait for AJ-E1705) | 2 |

Multiplied out, these come to millions of combinations. Nobody can walk
that many by hand, and most of them don't change behaviour. So:

### How each part is covered

- **Exhaustive where the logic is pure (L1).** These are enumerated in
  full and checked against the reference model in milliseconds:
  - **Membership and precedence**, one session against two pools:
    (7 matches × 2 scope × 3 capability × 2 sealed elsewhere × 2 matched
    twice)² = 28 224 cases.
  - **Talk decisions:** 4 sender roles × 4 receiver roles × 3 pair
    settings × 4 connection overrides × 2 inside/outside a sealed pool ×
    3 receiver capabilities × 4 receiver levels × 2 linked/unlinked pools
    = 9 216 cases.
  - **Claude's own channel per receiving session:** every mix of up to 3
    pools, each sealed, holding-ask or open = 27 cases, mapped to
    accept, hold or refuse.
  - **Migration:** every 0.3 pool kind × each kind of content it holds
    (members, messages, tasks, rules, approvals, docs, links, pauses,
    layout). After the move, the reference diff may show additions only.
- **All pairs where it's on screen (L3).** A deterministic all-pairs
  covering array (`scripts/qa/pairwise.py`, fixed seed, checked in)
  makes every pair of values appear together at least once. For the
  wizard that's about 60 rows, for the canvas about 40, and for every
  page and tab about 50. A coverage report lists each pair and the row
  that covers it.
- **Named risky triples on top.** Where three options meet in a way
  pairs can't catch, a row is written by hand. At least these:
  - sealed × several pools × a switch into a sealed App;
  - watched × ask first × Claude's own channel;
  - Account member × sign-in unreadable × a more specific member;
  - Full × waking × the same session in two pools;
  - Reviewer × per-member Read × a review task;
  - App member × the App signs in to another Account × an open session;
  - several projects × one task list × a project filter.
- **Boundaries everywhere.** Each list is tried at these sizes:
  - 0, 1, 8 and 9 items (search appears past 8);
  - 50 and 51 members (broad members draw as one group node);
  - 1 000+ ended sessions (keyset paging).

  The same goes for long names, both themes, 1024 and 1280 wide, and
  keyboard only.

## 4. Where the walks run

- **The Dev edition app.** It has its own data folder, so the real AI
  Juggler data is never touched.
- **The lab.** A throwaway HOME with spare lab accounts
  ([runtime-lab.md](runtime-lab.md)), for every live run: sessions really
  started, really messaging, really switched. Anyone's real accounts are
  read only.
- **The replica.** A HOME shaped like a heavy real Mac:
  - several Apps per Account, including Claude app profiles, one of them
    not running;
  - sessions started outside AI Juggler;
  - ended history in the thousands;
  - worktrees;
  - a project with no git.

  It's made by `just replica-oracle` and extended by AJ-E3301 for pools.
- **The release-candidate build** for the final pass. The built app, not
  `tauri dev`.

## 5. The use cases

Each scenario is a person with a goal, written as steps on screen. Each
step says what to look for and what to measure, and each scenario lists
the combinations it exercises. One sheet per scenario is kept in
`docs/qa/0.4-walks/` (§7). The templates' scenarios use the template, and
then a by-hand variant reaches the same pool without it.

### Pools (E30)

**P1 Writer and reviewer.** One person writes with Claude Code and has
Codex check it. This is the first template most people need.
1. New pool → Writer and reviewer. Scope: one project.
2. Pick the App Claude Code · Work as Lead and a Codex session as
   Reviewer. The side panel says what joins later.
3. Make the pool. The canvas shows both. The review step and the canvas
   say the same thing.
4. The writer claims a task and marks it in review. The Reviewer sends it
   back with a note, then approves it. Only the person can give the
   final Approve (D-44).
5. The writer's account runs out of room. Switch it to Codex · Personal.
   The switch check says it stays in the pool with full tools, and its
   tasks and messages come along.
6. A new Claude Code · Work session started in the project joins by
   itself.
7. A Claude app session in the project shows greyed in the add list,
   with its reason. Add it as Watched. Continue in AI Juggler, and it
   gets the tools.

Exercises: App member, auto-join, Reviewer, switching, watched to full.

**P2 Feature team.** A lead and three workers, each in its own worktree.
- Member to member is ask first. A held message shows in Needs you, with
  one count everywhere; Deliver and Refuse both work.
- Override one pair to allowed on the canvas. The edge label changes and
  the role-pair grid doesn't.
- The Lead gives a task out. A Member can't give one out, and is told
  why.
- Quit AI Juggler mid-way and reopen it: the pool, holds and tasks are
  all there.

**P3 Across repositories.** An API change in backend and its client in
web.
- Scope: two projects. The App member's sessions in both join by
  themselves.
- A session in a third project is refused with its reason, and "Add web
  to the scope" works in one click.
- Each task and card names its project, and the task list filters by
  project.

**P4 A reviewer for everything.** One Codex Reviewer across every
project; everyone in scope are Members.
- Member to member is blocked here.
- Each session is also in its project pool, so it is in two pools. The
  session page's Pools tab says how it got into each.
- The agent's writes go to the pool it names. A write to a pool it isn't
  in is refused.

**P5 Rules by account.** Everything on the Personal account is read only.
- The Account member is set to Read. A new session on another App of the
  same Account joins at Read.
- Name one Personal session on its own as Work together. It wins inside
  this pool.
- Make a sign-in unreadable (in the lab). The pool shows "Account
  unknown" and the session stays in through any other member.
- An App signs in to another Account. It leaves, and the pool says who
  left.

**P6 A build Mac.** Until AJ-E1705, the Mac member is offered and says
plainly that it waits for pools across Macs. Nothing pretends to work.
Once AJ-E1705 lands: the paired mini claims "run the full suite"; when
the mini goes offline its members show offline and messages queue.

**P7 Sealed client project.**
- Seal the pool. Its sessions leave their other pools, and elsewhere they
  show greyed: "in Client (sealed)".
- A message or doc out is refused, with its reason. Claude's own
  cross-session message into a sealed Claude session is refused. The lab
  proves this before it's built (AJ-E3002).
- Switching into an App the sealed pool holds warns first, and you
  confirm.
- Unsealing tells you what comes back.

**P8 Scouts.** Three to five short-lived sessions on mixed tools, one of
them OpenCode on its shell.
- All talk is allowed and the docs are shared.
- Ending sessions fade. Their tasks follow D-44.
- The shell-only member is labelled, and does what its shell allows.

**P9 Moving from 0.3.** Upgrade a copy of a 0.3 data folder that has
every kind of pool and content (§3, migration).
- Every pool, member, role, rule, message, task, approval and canvas
  position is still there.
- Project pools look the same, and user pools with no project now say
  "Every project".
- A backup was taken first.

**P10 Set up by hand, first time.** Someone who skips the wizard.
- On the empty canvas, the first step is the only primary button.
- They can make a working pool without the docs, in the order the
  canvas teaches.
- "Use a template" is still one click away.

**P11 Overlap and deletion.** Delete a pool that sessions were also in
elsewhere. They keep their other pools, or go back to their project pool.
History stays. Two pools at Full both wake one session: the rate limit
and the hop limit hold, and Stop all stops one pool only.

### Account and App (E32)

The eight things the audit found broken (§ "Eight things you'd do" in
`docs/research/entity-model-audit.md`), each now a scenario that must
pass:
- A1 Open a Claude app profile from the rail and land on its App page,
  with only its own sessions.
- A2 See everything on one subscription on the Account page: its Apps,
  sessions, projects and room.
- A3 From a session, open the App it runs in and the Account it's on,
  one click each.
- A4 See which projects an App was used for.
- A5 Find everything an out-of-room Account affects, from the Account
  page.
- A6 See three Claude app profiles on two subscriptions, including the
  one that isn't running (asked once, names only, with a line on the
  Privacy page).
- A7 Start Claude Code on the personal plan, choosing an App, not a
  folder.
- A8 Rename the work subscription. The new name shows everywhere: rail,
  pickers, notices, pools, ⌘K. Set its renewal once.

Then:
- A9 Upgrade: one Account per sign-in, Apps named by tool and profile.
  Where plan, renewal or fee disagree between folders, you choose once.
  Nothing is lost.
- A10 A sign-in can't be read: "Account unknown" shows in the right
  places, and counts leave it out with a note.

### Everything links (E31)

- **K1 The chain.** Home → a project → Apps (5) → Claude Code · Work →
  Sessions (open ones first) → a session → its Account chip → the
  Account page → Pools (1) → the canvas. Then back again, every hop one
  click, each count equal to the reference.
- **K2 Every page, every tab.** For each page and tab:
  - the count equals the list length, and both equal the reference;
  - the order is needs you, working, open, recent, rest;
  - search appears at 9 items;
  - every filter narrows correctly;
  - 1 000 ended sessions page without a stall;
  - new rows wait behind "3 new", so the list never jumps.
- **K3 Notices land.** Each kind of notice opens the right page and tab.
  The Needs you count equals the bell's.
- **K4 Pickers.** Each picker's top group fits where you are:
  - Open now, Recent, In this project, then All;
  - empty items last;
  - type-to-filter with arrows and Return.

### New session (E32)

- **N1 The usual.** It opens with the usual choice made and said in one
  sentence. ⌘↩ starts it, and the session really starts in that folder
  on that App.
- **N2 Change it.** Change folder and Change app are each one step away,
  and the sentence updates.
- **N3 The rare options.** They're folded, and the fold's label shows
  their current values. Changing one is remembered only if you say so.
- **N4 First time.** With no history, the choice is the best guess, and
  it says why.
- **N5 The usual is out of room.** It says so before you start and offers
  the App with room, never silently switching (D-31).

### Across features

- **X1 A day.** The morning's Needs you leads to a held pool message:
  approve it. An account runs out, so switch the session; its pool,
  tasks and messages follow. The notice names its pool. Mute the pool.
  Then:
  - clear the cache mid-walk: the page shows at once, the counts a
    moment later;
  - force-quit a session and then AI Juggler, and reopen;
  - at the end of the day, everything is where it was, and the usage
    lines match the vendor's own.
- **X2 Load.** A 50-member pool opens at once from the cached roster.
  Membership changes resolve without re-reading sessions; the debug log
  shows it.

## 6. The tools this needs (AJ-E3301)

- **Scenario files** (`docs/qa/scenarios/*.toml`). Each one states the
  lab and replica setup (Apps, Accounts, sessions, pools, members, rules)
  and the expected outcome per step. The same file feeds L2, L3 and the
  L4 sheet, so a walk and its automation can't drift apart.
- **The reference model** (`scripts/qa/pool_oracle.py`). It is written
  from D-62 and `docs/research/pools-model.md`, not from the Rust code,
  and has its own `--selftest`. It emits L1's expected tables and checks
  L2's harness output.
- **The pairwise generator** (`scripts/qa/pairwise.py`). Deterministic,
  with a coverage report, and the matrices are checked in.
- **Timing.** 0.3.2's IPC timing plus performance marks on each control.
  The walk sheet records click to feedback and click to done.
- **The walk sheet.** One row per step: what to do, what should show,
  what showed, ms, and a score of 1–5 for works, speed, clarity and
  layout. There's a column for the capture in each theme.
- **A lab setup script** that builds a scenario's starting state in the
  throwaway HOME, and tears it down afterwards (sessions stopped,
  processes reaped, no stray windows).

## 7. How a walk is recorded and judged

- One sheet per scenario in `docs/qa/0.4-walks/`. Each step has:
  - the expected and the seen;
  - the time;
  - the score;
  - captures, both themes, at 1024 and 1280, opened and checked by eye
    before commit.
- **Pass:** every step does what it promised end to end; nothing is
  scored under 4; feedback is ≤ 100 ms; nothing is cut off or overlaps.
- **A failure** becomes a finding on its task. It is fixed, and then the
  whole scenario is walked again from the start, not just the step that
  failed.
- The PR for a task links its sheets and says what was walked by hand.
